Policy Search Methods​

Instead of VVV and QQQ functions, we directly estimate the optimal policy.

πθ(a∣s)≈P(a∣s,θ)\pi^{\theta}(a|s) \approx P(a|s, \theta)πθ(a∣s)≈P(a∣s,θ)

  • Policy was generated directly from the value function (e.g. ϵ\epsilonϵ-greedy)
  • Parameterize the policy directly.
    • Instead of v(s,w)v(s,w)v(s,w) or q^(s,a,w)\hat{q}(s,a,w)q^​(s,a,w), we have πθ(a∣s)\pi^{\theta}(a|s)πθ(a∣s)
    • Given a particular state sss, what actions should we take? (What is probability of that particular action?)
    • θ\thetaθ is a learnable parameter which could be a neural network.

Value-based and Policy-based Methods​

  • Value-based: Learnt value function, Implicit policy (e.g. ϵ\epsilonϵ-greedy)
  • Policy-based: No value function, Learnt policy.
    • Good convergence properties
    • Effective in high-dimensional or continuous action spaces
      • if it is really continuous and large, it is not efficient as well.
    • Can learn stochastic policies.
    • Sometimes policies are relatively simple, values and models are complex.
    • Typically reaches local optima.
    • Obtained knowledge is specific and does not generalize well.
  • Actor-Critic: Learnt value function, Learnt policy.

Policy Gradient Techniques​

  • Trust-Region Based: Optimize the policy in a trust zone (closer circle).

Stochastic policies​

Rock-Paper-Scissors​

  • A deterministic policy is easily exploited
    • Deterministic policy: If we follow a set of action or sequence of actions, we will definitely reach the goal and will always reach teh same location.
  • A uniform random policy is optimal policy.

Actor-Critic​

  • Actor has a supervisor (Critic) that tells it how good or bad the action actor took is.
  • Actor: Controls agent's behavior using Policy-based methods.
  • Critic: Measures how good the action taken by the agent is using Value-based methods.

TRPO, Trust Region Policy Optimization​

A(s,a)=Q(s,a)−V(s)A(s,a) = Q(s,a) - V(s)A(s,a)=Q(s,a)−V(s)

  • Advantage: the difference between the Q and the V values.
    • How good is an action compared to the average action for a specific state.
  • Q(s,a)Q(s,a)Q(s,a) = the expected long-term return when taking action aaa in state sss
  • V(s)V(s)V(s) = the expected average long-term return when following the policy from state sss
  • Used in many Deep RL algorithms such as TRPO, PPO, GRP and etc.
  • Stationary Target: Fixed target during training.

Update the old policy to a new policy such that they are with in a "Trusted" distance apart.

  • Conservative policy update allows improvement instead of degradation of policy.
  • Improve training stability by avoiding parameter updates that changes the policy too much at one step.
  • By enforcing KL divergence constraint on size of the policy update at each iteration.

Kullback-Leibler Divergence​

KL divergence score

KL(P∣∣Q) KL(P || Q)KL(P∣∣Q)

  • It quantifies how much one probability distribution differs from another probability distribution.
  • ∣∣||∣∣ indicates divergence or PPP's divergence from QQQ.

DKL(P∣∣Q)=∑x∈XP(x)log⁡P(x)Q(x)D_{KL}(P || Q) = \sum_{x \in X} P(x) \log \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}DKL​(P∣∣Q)=∑x∈X​P(x)logQ(x)P(x)​

  • Given two probability distributions PPP and QQQ over a discrete random variable XXX, the KL divergence is the expected value under PPP of the log ratio between PPP and QQQ.
    • Multiplying by P(x)P(x)P(x) gives more weight to events that occur frequently under PPP.
    • PPP large, QQQ small → large divergence
    • PPP small, QQQ large → effect is smaller
  • e.g. πold(a1∣s)=0.8\pi_{\text{old}}(a_1|s) = 0.8πold​(a1​∣s)=0.8 and πold(a2∣s)=0.2\pi_{\text{old}}(a_2|s) = 0.2πold​(a2​∣s)=0.2, while πnew(a1∣s)=0.1\pi_{\text{new}}(a_1|s) = 0.1πnew​(a1​∣s)=0.1 and πnew(a2∣s)=0.9\pi_{\text{new}}(a_2|s) = 0.9πnew​(a2​∣s)=0.9
    • The policy changed drastically in one update step.
  • TRPO prevents this by enforcing DKL(πold∥πnew)≤δD_{KL}(\pi_{\text{old}} \| \pi_{\text{new}}) \leq \deltaDKL​(πold​∥πnew​)≤δ.

Policy Ratio​

rt(θ)=πθ(at∣st)πθold(at∣st)r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)} {\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t|s_t)}rt​(θ)=πθold​​(at​∣st​)πθ​(at​∣st​)​
  • rt(θ)=1r_t(\theta)=1rt​(θ)=1: no policy change
  • rt(θ)>1r_t(\theta)>1rt​(θ)>1: the action becomes more likely
  • rt(θ)<1r_t(\theta)<1rt​(θ)<1: the action becomes less likely

The surrogate objective is:

J(θ)=Et[rt(θ)A^t]J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_t \left[ r_t(\theta)\hat A_t \right]J(θ)=Et​[rt​(θ)A^t​]
  • A^t>0\hat A_t>0A^t​>0: the action was better than expected → increase its probability.
  • A^t<0\hat A_t<0A^t​<0: the action was worse than expected → decrease its probability.
  • Without a restriction, the policy may change too much in a single update.
